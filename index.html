Part 1: Single Point Detection

With the help of manually selected points from the IMM Face Database, we can turn keypoint selection into a regression problem. We train a basic convolutional network with three convolutional layers, some MaxPools, and two fully connected layers to detect the "nose" keypoint of the face.

Manually chosen nose keypoints from the IMM Database, used for training:

Training Process

In order to achieve high accuracy, we trained the model for 20 epochs at a learning rate of 0.005 with a batch size of 4. Convergence was unsteady but eventually the model achieved a validation loss below 0.002.

The final results were imperfect. The model did better than just guessing the mean keypoint, but it still struggled on images where a face was turned away from the camera. Neural nets' predictions (red) and actual values (yellow):

Part 2: Full Facial Keypoint Detection

We now trained the model on all of the facial keypoints. In order to reduce risk of overfitting, images were augmented--rotated, cropped, and brightened (pictured behind).

Model Details

A larger architecture was needed to support more learned features. Drawing inspiriation from the VCG-16 convolutional network, the model we used made use of doubled convolutional layers, followed by pools. Finally, two fully connected layers predicted the 58*2=116 keypoint features.

Training Process

A lower learning rate of 0.0005 as well as a very high number of epochs at 45 was used to achieve strong convergence. A lower batch size at 4 was found to be most helpful at reducing validation loss. Finally, a light (1e-7) weight decay was used in the Adam optimizer.

The final results were fairly good. The model did much better than predicting the mean face, and given the long training times, accuracy was fairly good. It is clear that the image augments made performance somewhat weaker, as the net tends to struggle on rotation images, as seen below. Neural nets' predictions (red) and actual values (yellow):

Part 3: Larger Datasets, Pretrained Models

Now we use data from the iBUG dataset, which is much larger that the IMM Face Database. Additionally, we use the pre-trained ResNet-18 model, which, true to its name, boasts 18 powerful layers.

Model & Training

The ResNet-18 model was pre-trained, with the final fully-connected layer being changed to output 68*2=136 features. From there, 50 additional epochs at a learning rate of 8e-3 and a batch size of 12 were used to fine-tune the model.

This model learned features very deeply and performed extremely well on the validation set. Neural nets' predictions (red) and actual values (yellow):

Part 4: Pixelwise Classification

We transform the regression into a classification problem! Instead of predicting which keypoint belongs where, we assign each pixel in the image with the probability a given keypoint belongs there. To do this, we convolve each keypoint with a Gaussian Kernel (size 21, std 10). This forms a 68 layer image——one layer for each keypoint

Model & Training

For our classifier we used the pretrained FCN-ResNet50 model. We manually changed the number of classes to be 68. Unfortunately, due to the lengthy time to generate heatmaps as well as the size of the model, it took extremely long to train this model——a single epoch took 2 hours. As such, only two epochs were gathered. To the side is a manually-constructed graph from data of two epochs, where the line with only three datapoints is the validation loss. One reason the validation loss is lower than the test loss here islikely because the validation data is not augmented.
